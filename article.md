### Statistics of Candy Land 

Candy Land is a game known as being just a game of chance, but there are some things that we can do to understand the probability that you get a specific card.

#### Distribution of colors
We can count the number of cards that are in a set and on the onset you might imagine that the numbers are evenly distributed, but they're not. Not only are there a different number of the colored cards, but the special cards add an extra variable so we first make a frequency table of the different options.


From this we can see the probability of drawing a specific color with replacement. We don't play the game with replacement so they're their actual probabilities change as you go, but we can use this as a way to have expected values. If we were playing with multiple decks the way that you would with blackjack, the statistics should be constant over time and replacement.

#### Distribution of Double Color and Single Color cards
In Candy Land, some cards have "double colors" which means the player advances to two spaces of the given color. What's the probability of getting a double versus a single color card?

If we count the special cards as singles then we have 22 double cards and 42 a single cards. That means approximately 1/3 of the time. When we play the game, we don't replace cards after they are drawn, but it gives a sense of the distribution.


I drew ten collections of cards five times to get this frequency chart. S=Single color, D=Double Color. In any one grouping, the distribution is different than the 33% expected, but as a group, doubles came up 34% of the time.

#### Still just a game of chance?
We make the game more difficult by requiring the player to make a prediction about the color or type of card that will be drawn and only advancing if their guess is correct.
